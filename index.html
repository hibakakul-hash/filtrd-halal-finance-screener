Shariah-compliant mutual funds currently available for Indian investors. These funds are screened by recognised Shariah boards and invest only in halal-compliant equities.

India — SEBI-Registered Shariah Mutual Funds

Note on returns: Halal mutual funds exclude financial sector stocks (banks, conventional insurance, NBFCs), which are ~35% of Nifty 500 by weight. This means halal funds may underperform broad indices in periods when financial stocks lead. However, they have historically outperformed during financial crises.

Digital Gold — Shariah Status in India
Digital Gold platforms (Sovereign Gold Bond, DigiGold, SafeGold, MMTC-PAMP): Most digital gold platforms in India are NOT Shariah-compliant because (a) they do not guarantee delivery of the specific unit you purchased, violating the requirement for specific ownership, (b) SGBs pay interest (riba), and (c) gold ETFs involve indirect ownership not certified by any Shariah board.
What IS halal: Purchasing physical gold coins or bars from a jeweller or certified refinery, paid spot, with immediate physical delivery. Store in a bank locker or home safe. Avoid making charges-based jewellery as investment.

Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) — Islamic Ruling
SGBs pay 2.5% annual interest — this is riba and makes SGBs haram. Even though the principal tracks gold price (which is permissible), the guaranteed interest component prohibits them. Scholars generally agree on this ruling without exception.

Allowed (Halal) Forms of Investment

Islam encourages wealth creation and investment. The following are permitted — and some are strongly recommended — for Muslims.

Sukuk — Islamic Bonds (Simple Explanation)

Sukuk are the Islamic alternative to conventional interest-bearing bonds. They are currently not available in India, but are widely used in Malaysia, GCC countries, and increasingly in the UK.
